Staffing Solutions · For facilities

What to include in a staffing request.

Unit, shift, specialty, start date. Send what you have and we'll ask about the rest. This page lists what helps us start on a match right away.

Request Staffing

The essentials

Four things every request needs.

These four details are what we ask for on every request. With them, we can start looking for the professional who fits.

  1. 1

    The unit

    Where the professional will work. The unit tells us about the patient population, the pace, and the kind of experience that fits.

  2. 2

    The shift

    Days, nights, weekends, or a rotation, and the hours. Availability is one half of a match; the schedule you actually need is the other.

  3. 3

    The specialty

    The role and the specialty, for example a Registered Nurse for a skilled nursing unit or a Physical Therapist for rehab. If more than one type of professional could fill the role, say so.

  4. 4

    The start date

    When coverage needs to begin. If the date is flexible, a range is fine. If it is fixed, tell us and we will work back from it.

Helpful if you have it

Details that make the match better.

None of these are required. Each one helps us look for the right person instead of an available one.

  • Which kind of staffing

    Whether you are looking for a per-diem shift, a contract placement for a defined period, or a permanent hire. If you are not sure, describe the need and we will talk it through.

  • How long you expect the need to last

    A single shift, a few weeks, a season, or open ended. This shapes who we look for.

  • Who we should talk to

    The best person to reach about this request, and the best way to reach them. Requests move faster when the questions go to the right desk.

  • Anything about the team

    What has worked well with staff in the past, what has not, and what the people already on the unit need from the person joining them. This is the part a requisition never captures, and it is the part we match on.
